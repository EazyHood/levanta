# Round 16: the leak is a height rule, and blocking on it still loses

Round 15 concluded that the leak front "sits on no fitted plane, so the problem is before
the height rule". **That was one measurement too early.** There are two height gates, and
counting only one of them undercounted the loss.

## 1. Where a candidate wall actually dies

The same 399 cells (real wall, not drawn), against every gate in `extract_faces`, by
re-running its loop with a counter at each `continue`:

| where the candidate died | runs | metres | covers the leak front |
|---|---|---|---|
| too few height bands (< 4.9 of 14.1) | 85 | 65.6 m | **32 %** |
| does not reach the ceiling | 22 | 32.8 m | **31 %** |
| too short (< 0.40 m) | 488 | 76.6 m | 11 % |
| accepted as a face | 159 | 312.9 m | 3 % |

**Height accounts for 63 % of the front**, split almost evenly between two different rules.
Length accounts for 11 %, and the remaining ~23 % never gathered five points on a line at
all. So the pre-check the supervisor asked for does pass: there is something to grab, and it
is the runs that were long enough to be a wall and failed only on height.

Both rules are deliberate. The ceiling rule is documented in `extract_faces` as what
separates a wall from a wardrobe or an open door leaf, and the README's "tall furniture
looks like a wall" is the failure it exists to prevent.

## 2. So the blocker was built from exactly those runs, and the bench still says no

`extract_faces` now takes a `low` list and collects the runs it discards on height;
`_face_raster` turns runs into cells; `free_space_raster` cuts each ray at the first one.
Three versions, all measured over the seven scenes:

| what stops the ray | mean area error | wall recall | rooms | TUM example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **nothing (today)** | **17 %** | **31 %** | 6 of 8 | 24.5 m² (+0 %) |
| any cell with a wall point (round 14) | 21 % | 25 % | 6 of 8 | 22.4 m² (−9 %) |
| every fitted run, accepted or not | 22 % | 25 % | 6 of 8 | 22.4 m² (−9 %) |
| **only the runs too low to be a wall** | 20 % | 25 % | 6 of 8 | **24.5 m² (+0 %)** |

The sharpest version is the best of the three and still loses. It does keep the published
TUM example intact, which the other two broke, and it does shrink the culprit room. It also
takes 42897526 from 4 walls and −7 % to 1 wall and −29 %: a small bathroom's fixtures are
precisely the runs that get discarded on height, so blocking there starves the free space
the room is built from.

**Three rejections of the same lever.** The default stays off. What the round leaves behind
is the machinery any future attempt needs, with the reason each version failed written next
to it, and one number that says where a fourth attempt would have to work: the 23 % of the
front that never gathers five points on a line is not a threshold problem at all.

## 3. A check the reorder needed

Face extraction had to move above the raster block, since the rays now need the runs. With
the option off, the seven-scene table is **identical to round 11's**, field by field. A
reorder that changes nothing is worth proving rather than assuming.
