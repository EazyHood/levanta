# Round 18: bridging is cheap, and cutting everywhere is not

Round 17 ended with "a fifth attempt needs the fragments closed into a boundary". The
supervisor's warning came with it: closing means bridging over border nobody filmed, and a
room with an invented wall measures well while saying more than it knows. So the measurement
had to be *how much* gets invented.

**The prediction, written before running:** bridging at 1 m takes the mean per-room error
under 100 %, and the bridged share of the perimeter comes out at 35-50 %, well above the
23 % of border that never gathered five points on a line, because bridges are needed between
fragments of the same wall too. Worth it means under 60 % error with at most 25 % bridged.

## 1. Wrong, and in the useful direction

On the flat, joining evidence ends within `d` and cutting the rooms with the result:

| | rooms | area | per-room error | mean \|e\| | outline on evidence | **bridged** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| as it is | 2 | +20 % | −65, +226, +236 | 175 % | 66 % | 0 % |
| bridged ≤ 0.5 m | **3** | +15 % | −65, +152, −39 | **86 %** | 72 % | **1 %** |
| bridged ≤ 1.0 m | 3 | +14 % | −65, +151, −40 | 86 % | 72 % | 5 % |
| bridged ≤ 1.5 m | 4 | +11 % | −66, +123, −43 | 77 % | 64 % | 11 % |
| bridged ≤ 2.0 m | 4 | +7 % | −69, +123, −45 | 79 % | 53 % | 22 % |

**Bridging is far cheaper than predicted: 1 % of the perimeter at 0.5 m, not 35-50 %.** And
the error does fall under 100 %, to 86 %, which is exactly the "separate rooms" value of the
bimodal split. So the bridge does not beat the good mode; **it reaches it**, turning the
fused mode into the separate one. Round 12 said the fusion is what decides whether a plan is
usable, and this is the first thing that has moved it.

## 2. And the bench rejects it anyway, for a new reason

Over the seven scenes, with the clip applied to every room:

| | mean area error | wall recall | rooms matched |
|---|---|---|---|
| **as it is** | **17 %** | **31 %** | 6 of 8 |
| clipped, bridging at 0.5 m | 39 % | 19 % | **7 of 8** |

The best room structure any attempt has produced, and the worst area error. Every
single-room scene loses twenty points or more (42897526 goes to −67 %, the TUM example to
−16 %) because the same cut fires inside rooms that never needed cutting.

That is a different failure from the previous four. Those did nothing or notched; this one
cuts, and cuts where nothing was wrong.

**So the sixth attempt is not a better fence. It is a decision about where to apply it:**
clip only the rooms suspected of having fused. The bench can define that suspicion with
ground truth; a plan on a user's machine cannot yet, and finding a signal that can is the
next question.

## 3. What this says about the dashed line

The product idea behind the round survives the rejection: a closed outline drawn differently
where it was bridged. **Bridging costs 1 % of the perimeter at 0.5 m and 22 % at 2.0 m**, so
a sheet that dashes the bridged stretches would be honest at a very small cost in ink. What
is missing is not the drawing, it is knowing which rooms to cut.

One correction along the way: the first pipeline version kept only the largest walked piece
after a cut, which throws away the room the cut had just separated. The scratch measurement
kept them all, and the two disagreed until that was fixed. Keeping every walked piece is
what the flat's third room depends on.
