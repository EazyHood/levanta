# Round 10: where the exact-depth error is introduced

## 0. A correction to round 9 first, because it moves the question

Round 9 reported that exact depth still loses 21 % of the area. **That number was about
coverage, not about the planner.** It was measured on 48 of the walk's 116 views, so half
the flat was never seen. With all 116:

| exact depth, exact poses | rooms | walls | area |
|---|---|---|---|
| 48 views | 2 | 5 | 41.2 m² (−21 %) |
| **116 views** | **3** | **17** | **63.3 m² (+22 %)** |

So there is no −21 % to explain. The second ceiling is real but it has the shape round 7
already published: the total comes out **over** the truth while the rooms are wrong
individually (−73 %, +161 %, −28 %), a mean per-room error of **87 %**.

## 1. The ablation, and the threshold written before running it

Each stage of the planner is switched off in turn on the flat's perfect cloud, and judged
room by room, not on the total. **A stage owns the error if switching it off brings the
mean per-room error below 60 %, or gives three rooms each within 40 % of its truth.**
Anything less than that and the error is spread, not localised.

Every row is the flat's perfect cloud, scored room by room against each room's own truth.

| what is switched off | rooms matched | per-room error | mean \|error\| |
|---|---|---|---|
| **nothing (today)** | 3 of 3 | −73 %, +161 %, −28 % | **87 %** |
| line of sight (`max_rays=0`) | 2 of 3 | −75 %, +134 %, missed | worse |
| the seen-floor fallback | **0 of 3** | all missed | worst |
| the Manhattan rounding | 3 of 3 | −66 %, +135 %, −28 % | 76 % |
| the wall cleanup (`tidy`) | 3 of 3 | −73 %, +161 %, −28 % | **87 %, identical** |
| a stricter interior test | 3 of 3 | −73 %, +161 %, −21 % | 85 % |
| the 1 m closing, swept down to 0.15 m | 3 of 3 | −73 %, +154 %, −28 % | 85 % |
| the sight-line guard, swept 0.5 → 0.1 m | 3 of 3 | −73 %, +138 %, −28 % | 82 % |

**No stage owns it.** The threshold is met by none of them, and two results are worth
saying out loud: the wall cleanup changes *nothing at all* on this scene, and without the
seen-floor fallback there are **no rooms**, because the closed-pocket stage finds none.

## 2. Two oracle ablations, which do find it

If no switch explains the error, replace a stage's *input* with the truth.

| | rooms | total | per-room error | mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| today | 3 | 61.5 m² (+19 %) | −73 %, +161 %, −28 % | 87 % |
| **cut the interior with the real walls** | 3 | 61.4 m² (+19 %) | −68 %, +155 %, −28 % | **84 %** |
| **clip the interior to the real floor** | 3 | 23.3 m² (−55 %) | −73 %, −63 %, −28 % | **54 %** |

The real walls remove only 8.9 m² of the 41.4 m² interior and change nothing: **wall
detection is not the ceiling.** Clipping to the real floor removes **20.6 m², half the
interior**, and the +161 % room collapses to −63 %.

So the answer to "where does the error come from" is not a stage, it is the interior
estimate itself, and it is wrong in two opposite directions at once:

- **it leaks out of the building.** Half of what levanta calls interior is not floor of this
  flat. Sight lines leave through doorways and windows and mark free space outside, and that
  outside strip is what joins rooms around the walls and inflates one to +161 %;
- **it is thin where it is right.** Clip the leak away and every room is too small
  (−73 %, −63 %, −28 %), because floor points reach only 36 % of the real floor.

That is why the total looked good: the leak inflates by roughly what the thin coverage
loses. It also explains round 8's result, where the wall-first builder lost on totals: it
does not leak, so it has nothing to cancel the shortfall with.

## 3. What ships from this round

The measured numbers are now on the README's front page, before the install section: one
room comes out 17-31 % small, a three-room flat came out as one room and 43 % small, and
even with perfect depth the three rooms are −73 %, +161 % and −28 %. A reader deserves the
figure, not a generic warning.

No planner change was spent. `free_guard` is now a `PlanOptions` knob at exactly its old
value (0.5 m) so the bench can sweep it; it is not a behaviour change, and the sweep says
it is not the lever either.

**Not done: the second depth network.** It was conditional on the ablation, and the
ablation says the planner's ceiling is not depth at all: with *exact* depth the rooms are
still −73 %, +161 % and −28 %. A second network would move the reconstruction ceiling that
round 9 characterised, not this one. It needs a model download and a GPU stint, and it
should be judged against round 9's numbers, not these.

## 4. The published example that was wrong in three ways

Jhona pointed at the U2 apartment sheet in the repository. It was wrong three times over,
and only one of them was cosmetic:

- **it was in Spanish**, in an English-first public repository, and mixed at that: the room
  names read *Room 1* while everything around them read *Cuadro de áreas* and *incompleta*;
- **the notes ran off the right edge of the paper** and were cut mid-word;
- **the sheet published two different floor areas.** The title block said 30.99 m², the area
  schedule beside it said 30.55 m². Both were computed correctly and neither was a rounding
  error: rooms 2 and 5 **overlap by 0.44 m²**, the title block summed the rooms and the
  schedule took their union.

The area is now the union everywhere, which is the honest number, and an overlap is
**reported as a check** rather than absorbed silently: *"Two rooms share 0.44 m² of floor."*
The sheet is regenerated in English, the notes wrap, and the example is finally listed in
`examples/README.md` with the command that regenerates it, language flag included.

The example also gained the *PRELIMINARY · scale not calibrated* stamp, which the committed
version predated: it comes from video with no measured door, so it should always have
carried it.
