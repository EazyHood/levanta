# Round 8: the floor is a thin source, and inverting the hierarchy does not fix it

Round 7 ended with a number that reframes the project: on a walked flat, with **exact
depth and exact poses**, floor points reach only **36 %** of the real floor, even though
the camera path passes within 2 m of 80-94 % of every room. If the floor is that thin, it
cannot be the base of the outline. This round tests that idea, and reports the coverage to
the user whatever the answer.

## 1. The +161 % room is not the storey above

`apartment_0` has a second floor 2.85 m up, so the obvious suspect was contamination.
Measured instead of assumed:

- levanta's own wall band already stops at **2.21 m** (the ceiling it measures), below the
  upper floor at 2.85 m, so upper-storey walls never enter the wall detection;
- cutting the cloud at 2.5 m drops 9.1 % of the points and moves the room **not at all**
  (44.6 → 44.6 m²); cutting at 2.2 m makes it worse (57.4 m²).

| the 44.6 m² room, against a true room of 17.1 m² | |
|---|---|
| on the lower floor | 14.4 m² |
| only on the storey above | 10.8 m² |
| on neither, i.e. outside the building at both levels | **19.4 m²** |
| of true room 1 / 2 / 3 it covers | 4.0 / 7.6 / 2.9 m² |

So it spans all three rooms *and* leaves the building. It is not a height problem: it is
the interior being inferred as "seen floor **or** free space the camera looked through",
and the line of sight goes out of the flat.

## 2. Walls first instead of floor first: built, measured, not adopted

`src/levanta/plan/wall_rooms.py` inverts the two sources. Every wall line becomes a full
cut across the plan; the cuts make a rectangular arrangement (the frame is Manhattan); each
rectangle asks the floor only whether it is interior; and two interior rectangles join
unless a wall body stands between them. A doorway is a gap in the wall body, so it joins
two rectangles with no bridging heuristic, and every outline is closed and orthogonal
because it is a union of rectangles.

Over the six scenes levanta stands behind:

| | mean total error | mean per-room error | worst room | rooms matched |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **floor first (the default)** | 19.9 % | **52.7 %** | 161 % | **7 of 8** |
| walls first | **19.5 %** | 78.2 % | 189 % | 6 of 8 |
| walls first, edges blocked at 25 % | 21.3 % | 61.8 % | 157 % | 7 of 8 |

**It ties on the total and loses room by room, so the default does not move.** And it loses
in exactly the way round 7 warned about: on 47331964 it turns −21 % into **+11 %** while
the single room it finds goes from −28 % to **+72 %**. A year ago that scene would have
looked like a win.

Why it loses is not a bug: it cannot invent a wall that was never detected, and wall recall
is 14-48 %. It stays in the tree behind `PlanOptions.rooms_from="walls"`, with tests, to be
re-measured when wall recall improves.

## 3. The number the user gets whatever the planner does

Every room now carries `floor_seen`: the share of its outline resting on floor a camera
actually saw. It appears next to the room name on the sheet, in the area schedule, and as a
check when it falls under a half. The TUM office reads **"Office (incomplete · 56 % on seen
floor)"**; the synthetic apartment reads 100 %.

Getting that number honest took three attempts, and the control was the synthetic
apartment, whose floor is modelled in full and swept from every side, so it *must* score
~100 %:

| rule | synthetic (control) | Replica flat | ARKitScenes 47331964 |
|---|---|---|---|
| count the 2 cm cells holding a floor point | **30 %** ✗ | 30-33 % | 1-4 % |
| a 10 cm square holds at least one point | **100 %** ✓ | 61-75 % | 3-23 % |
| a 10 cm square is at least a quarter full | **59 %** ✗ | 47-53 % | 0-11 % |

Counting raw cells measures the 2 cm voxel spacing. Requiring the square to be a quarter
full measures point density. A fourth attempt, growing a 10 cm disc around every point,
scored the Replica flat at 82 % and contradicted a direct measurement of that same scene
against its mesh: one lone point became a hundred cells. **The first version of this was
already written to the sheet before the control caught it.**

## 4. The rule held

One planner change was available this round and the measurement said not to spend it, so
the default builder is untouched. What shipped is the coverage the reader gets, which
changes no geometry.
