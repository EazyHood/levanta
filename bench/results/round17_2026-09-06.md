# Round 17: it was never the order, it was that fragments are not a boundary

Three attempts had stopped the sight lines *during* the tracing and all three cost the same
six points of wall recall. The reading was that free space cannot both find the interior and
respect its border, and that the fix was to let the tracing run and **cut afterwards**,
using the evidence round 16 measured: the runs face extraction discards on height, which
cover 63 % of the front where the interior leaves the building.

It was built, and it loses too. But the reason is not the one the attempt was based on.

## 1. Clipping the finished rooms with the low runs

`PlanOptions.rooms_clipped_by_low_walls`, off by default. Over the seven scenes:

| | mean area error | wall recall | rooms |
|---|---|---|---|
| **as it is** | **17 %** | **31 %** | 6 of 8 |
| best blocker (low runs, round 16) | 20 % | 25 % | 6 of 8 |
| rooms clipped with the low runs | **32 %** | 24 % | 6 of 8 |

And on the flat, the culprit room goes from **55.8 to 55.1 m²**. It is not cut, it is
notched. Meanwhile a small bathroom drops from 4.0 to 1.9 m² (−66 %) because there a blade
does happen to cross the room, and the largest surviving fragment is most of it thrown away.

## 2. The order was never the problem

Round 10's oracle clipped the interior **raster** to the real floor and reached 33 %. If the
order mattered, doing the same thing to the finished **polygons** should do worse. It does
not:

| on the flat, mean per-room error | |
|---|---|
| as it is | 175 % |
| clipped with the low runs | 175 % |
| interior raster clipped to the real floor | **33 %** |
| finished polygons clipped to the real floor | **40 %** |

Clipping afterwards is as good as clipping before. **What separates the two working rows
from the two failing ones is not when the cut happens, it is that the real floor is a closed
region and the runs are fragments.** Subtracting a fragment from a polygon makes a notch;
only a cut that crosses from one side to the other separates anything.

That closes the line: four attempts, two orders, three criteria, one cause. A fifth does not
need a better rule for which fragments to trust. It needs the fragments **closed into a
boundary** — which is the wall round 6 hit from the other side, where the closed-room stage
found two pockets in a three-room flat and closed none, because real walls never form
complete circuits.

## 3. The part that no algorithm reaches, now on the front page

Of the border where the interior leaves the building: 63 % is wall levanta saw and judged
too low to draw, 11 % is too short to fit, and **23 % never gathered five points on a line
at all**. That last quarter is border nobody filmed. No threshold, no sight-line rule and no
clipping reaches it, and the README now says so in those terms, because it is the share of
this problem that belongs to the capture rather than to the code.
