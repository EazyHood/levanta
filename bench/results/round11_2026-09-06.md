# Round 11: the bench was measuring the planner twice

## 0. Three numbers for one quantity, and then a fourth reason they differed

The supervisor caught me quoting **+22 %** in a message and **+19 %** in the README for what
looked like the same row. Both were mine, both were right, and neither was labelled:

| what was fused and planned | area |
|---|---|
| 58 views, every second one of the walk, voxel 0.03 — **the published cloud** | +19 % |
| all 116 views, voxel 0.03 | +23 % |
| all 116 views, voxel 0.02 | +22 % |

Chasing that down turned up something worse than a labelling slip.

## 1. The published cloud was the planner's own output

`bench/ideal_input.py` saved `res.cloud`, which `extract_floor_plan` had already
gravity-aligned, Manhattan-rotated and voxel-thinned. The bench then **replanned that file**.
So the flagship "perfect cloud" row was the planner running on its own output, twice, and a
user only ever gets one pass.

Same 58 frames, same fusion, the only difference being how many times the planner sees them:

| | rooms | matched | area | per-room error | mean \|error\| |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **one pass** (the fused cloud) | 2 | 2 of 3, one covering two | 62.1 m² (+20 %) | −65 %, +226 %, +236 % | **175 %** |
| two passes (its own output) | 3 | 3 of 3 | 61.5 m² (+19 %) | −73 %, +161 %, −28 % | **87 %** |
| the file that was published | 3 | 3 of 3 | 61.5 m² (+19 %) | −73 %, +161 %, −28 % | 87 % |

**The second pass halved the apparent error.** It is not noise: four random seeds move the
one-pass result by less than a point (84-85 % mean error on the 116-view cloud), so the
difference is the extra pass itself.

The bench now reads the cloud that goes *into* the planner: `fused_cloud.ply` for the flat,
and `plan_recon.ply` — which was already on disk — for the five ARKitScenes rooms. The
corrected table:

Totals got *better* (mean absolute area error 17 % against 20 %) and the room structure got
worse (6 of 8 rooms matched against 7). The README front page now carries the one-pass
numbers and says which they are.

## 2. The coverage curve: it does not saturate, and it is not even monotonic

Prefixes of the walk, exact depth and poses, the canonical fusion. The prediction written
before running was that the room count saturates near 60 views and the per-room error keeps
falling. **Both halves are wrong.**

| views | walked | rooms found | fused | area | per-room error | mean \|e\| | floor seen |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 5 m | 1 of 3 | 0 | 8.0 m² (−85 %) | −56 % | 56 % | 12 % |
| 40 | 8 m | 2 of 3 | 0 | 33.8 m² (−35 %) | −60 %, +56 % | 58 % | 21 % |
| 60 | 14 m | 1 of 3 | **1** | 19.3 m² (−63 %) | +7 %, +16 % | 11 % | 34 % |
| 80 | 17 m | 2 of 3 | **1** | 63.0 m² (+22 %) | +154 %, +168 %, −28 % | 117 % | 37 % |
| 116 | 24 m | 3 of 3 | 0 | 63.6 m² (+23 %) | −64 %, +163 %, −27 % | 85 % | 46 % |

Rooms found go 1, 2, 1, 2, 3. Seen floor climbs steadily, from 12 % to 46 %, and it is the
only column that behaves. The mean per-room error is **not readable on its own**: the 11 %
at 60 views is one fused room credited against two true rooms of similar size, which is why
the table now carries a `fused` column.

So the capture guide cannot say "walk 60 metres and stop". What it can say is what the
`floor seen` column shows: **more walking buys more observed floor, monotonically**, and 24
metres of walking in a 52 m² flat still only reaches 46 % of its floor.

## 3. The confidence signals do not predict the error

Every sheet already prints how much of a room's outline rests on seen floor, and now warns
when two rooms share floor. Both need no ground truth, so both are available in a user's
house. Do they say which room to distrust?

| | rank correlation with \|error\| |
|---|---|
| share of the outline on seen floor | **+0.18** (a useful warning would be strongly negative) |
| share shared with another room | +0.29 (right sign, weak) |
| shared floor against off-the-building area | +0.29 (round 10's hypothesis: not confirmed) |

And the plain comparison, over the seven scored rooms:

| | seen floor | shared |
|---|---|---|
| rooms wrong by more than 50 % | **72 %** | 0 % |
| the rest | **34 %** | 4 % |

**The badly wrong rooms had twice as much seen floor as the rest.** In hindsight it is
obvious: a room that swallows its neighbour is enormous and rests on plenty of evidence, in
the wrong shape. Seven rooms is a hint and not a result, but it is enough to stop anyone
building a reliability score on that number, so the README now says explicitly that the
percentage describes what was observed and is not a trust score.
