different definitions.

## 2. A real flat in CI

`tests/test_apartment_gate.py` runs the three-room flat end to end on every push. The
cloud is thinned to 40 046 points, which changes nothing (16 walls and 61.5 m² at full
density, 16 and 61.6 m² thinned), and the thresholds are wide enough that only a real
change moves them: three rooms, 45–78 m², at least twelve walls, the longest at least 9 m.

The cloud itself is **not** in the repository: it derives from Replica's mesh, whose
licence is for research, and levanta is MIT. Whoever has the dataset generates it with
three commands in the test's docstring, and the tests start running; without it they skip
and the seven-scene bench is what guards the planner. So this gate protects whoever
develops with the data at hand, not the CI runner, which is the honest limit of a
non-redistributable dataset.

## 3. The rooms-need-walls circle, attacked and measured

`build_rooms` now reports how it found the rooms (`debug.rooms`). On the flat, with a
perfect cloud:

    {'pockets': 2, 'too_small': 0, 'leaks_out': 1, 'not_inside': 1,
     'closed': 0, 'stage2': 1, 'fallback': 2}

**Two pockets in a three-room flat, and none closed.** Subtracting wall bodies from a box
only encloses anything where the walls form a complete circuit, which real walls never do;
every pocket leaks to the outside and the rooms fall back to the seen floor, which is why
they fuse.

Two ways out were measured, both worse:

| | flat (truth 51.8 m²) | 47331964 (truth 27.8 m²) | TUM (ref 24.5 m²) |
|---|---|---|---|
| today | +19 % | −21 % | +0 % |
| rebuild rooms from the cleaned walls | +40 % | −65 % | +9 %, one spurious room |
| bridge 1.2 m gaps instead of 0.6 m | +15 % | −45 % | +0 % |

Rebuilding after the cleanup is worse because the cleanup *removes* walls, so there are
fewer obstacles. Wider bridging trades one scene for another. The circle is real and the
fix is not a constant; what the instrumentation now makes possible is attacking it with
the pocket counts in front of you.

## 4. Geometry or placement: answered

`bench/known_poses.py`, on the rendered walk where both are exact.

**The depth the network predicts**, against the renderer's own depth, over 94 views:

| | |
|---|---|
| median predicted / true | **0.54** (p10 0.37, p90 **0.70**) |
| abs-rel error once each view's own scale is divided out | median **0.056**, mean 0.099 |

The *shape* the network predicts is good to about 6 %. Its *scale* is half the truth and,
far more damaging, **swings by a factor of two from view to view**.

**The same frames with their true poses handed to the network:**

| | rooms | walls | area error | camera RMS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| normal reconstruction | 1 of 3 | 5 | −49 % | 1.08 m |
| with the exact focal length | 2 of 3 | 8 | −38 % | 1.04 m |
| **with exact poses** | **1 of 3** | **3** | **−43 %** | 1e-7 m |
| perfect cloud (exact depth too) | 3 of 3 | 16 | +19 % | 1e-7 m |

The threshold written before running was: with true poses, two rooms of three and area
within ±15 % would mean placement was the binding constraint. **It is not met, and not
narrowly**: perfect poses change nothing, while perfect *depth* changes everything.

So the drift that rounds 4 and 5 chased with pose graphs, ICP and chunk composition was
never a pose problem. It is per-view scale in the network's depth, and the next lever is
the one that follows from that: estimate a scale per *view* from its overlap with its
neighbours, which is what `align_chunk` already does one level up, between chunks.
