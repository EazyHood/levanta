# Round 13: the 85 % is not a ceiling, it is a fixed triple

Round 12 found the error is bimodal: 85 % per room when the three rooms come out separate,
175-223 % when two of them fuse, nothing in between. That changes how the result has to be
reported and what is worth fixing.

## 1. There is no average to quote

Ten runs of the same flat with exact depth and poses, one pass:

| | how often | per-room error |
|---|---|---|
| the three rooms come out separate | 3 of 6 sampling configurations | −65 %, −27 %, +162 % |
| two of them merge into one | 3 of 6 | 175-223 % |

The average of the two describes a state that never occurs, so the README now publishes
both behaviours and how often each happened, rather than one number.

## 2. And the good mode is not a blur either

The 85 % looked like a stable ceiling. It is not a ceiling, it is the mean of a **fixed
triple** that repeats to within a few points across every run that gets three rooms:

| the room | its error | spread over four runs |
|---|---|---|
| 18.1 m² | **−65 %** | 3.6 points |
| 16.6 m² | **−27 %** | 1.0 points |
| 17.1 m² | **+162 %** | 2.6 points |

It is always the same room that comes out at two and a half times its size, and the total
lands at +21 to +23 % only because the other two are short. This is the third time in this
project a stable, respectable-looking number has turned out to be errors of opposite sign
cancelling, and it is why the per-room columns exist.

So there are two problems and they are not the same one:

- **the fusion** decides whether the plan is usable at all;
- **the +162 % room** is a single, reproducible misassignment, and no amount of extra
  footage moves it: it is identical at 40, 80 and 116 views.

## 3. Two mechanisms proposed for the +162 % room, both false

Its area is 54 % off the flat's floor, so the question is where it leaves the building.

**Is it a thin sight-line finger through a doorway?** No. The off-floor part is a single
contiguous piece of 16.1 m² of the 16.4 m² inside the truth grid, plus 13.5 m² beyond the
grid entirely. Eroding the room by 0.10, 0.20, 0.30, 0.40, 0.50 and 0.70 m never separates
it into pieces: it stays one component, 61 % of it on the flat at every radius. **There is
no neck to cut.** The leak is joined to the flat along a broad front.

**Is it rays reaching points on the storey above?** `free_space_raster` traces to every
point, ceiling included, and a ray to something 3 m up crosses plan cells well outside the
lower floor. Cutting the cloud at the ceiling before planning makes it **worse**, not
better:

| cloud | rooms | area | mean per-room \|e\| |
|---|---|---|---|
| as it is | 2 | +20 % | 175 % |
| points above 2.5 m dropped (9 %) | 2 | +27 % | 191 % |
| above 2.2 m dropped (14 %) | 1 | +11 % | 240 % |
| above 1.9 m dropped (22 %) | 1 | +11 % | 240 % |

Those points carry the wall evidence, and removing them costs more than the leak does.

That first test also had to be run twice: the height filter was applied with the
aligned-cloud transform to a raw cloud that was already in the mesh frame, so it dropped
0 % of the points and reported "no effect" for a run. The tell was the 0 %.

What is left standing is round 10's oracle: clipping the interior to the real floor removes
half of it and brings the mean per-room error to 33 %. The leak is real and it dominates,
but it is not a finger and it is not the storey above, so the next attempt has to work on
what makes `free_r` believe a broad region outside the building is interior.
