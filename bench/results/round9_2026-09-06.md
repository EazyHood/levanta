# Round 9: the per-view scale can be recovered, unless the shape is 5 % wrong

Rounds 6 and 7 both ended pointing at the same lever. The network's poses are not the
problem, its shape is good to 5.6 %, and what breaks the reconstruction is that each view's
depth carries its own scale. So: estimate a scale per view from its overlap with its
neighbours, the way `align_chunk` already does one level up between chunks.

It does not work, and the reason is not the one anybody would have guessed.

## The idea, and why it is a linear system

If view *i* and view *j* see the same surface point, that point has to land in one place:

    R_i (s_i d_i r_i) + t_i  =  R_j (s_j d_j r_j) + t_j

which is linear in the two scales, with the baseline on the right. Every correspondence
gives three rows, so a whole walk's scales come out of one least-squares solve.
`bench/per_view_scale.py` builds it; thresholds were written into its docstring before the
first run: the corrected/true spread must fall from 1.8 to **≤ 1.15** and the median error
of a view must be **≤ 5 %**.

## What it can and cannot survive

The control is the rendered walk, where the depth is exact and the error is injected, so
the estimator is asked to recover something known. 48 views.

| what the depth carries | how the matches are found | spread of corrected/true | median error of a view |
|---|---|---|---|
| a scale error only | perfect (from the true depth) | **1.059** | **0.9 %** |
| a scale error only | optical flow on the images | 1.334 | 5.3 % |
| a scale error **and 5.6 % shape error** | perfect | 1.396 | 10.1 % |
| a scale error **and 5.6 % shape error** | optical flow | 2.142 | 14.2 % |
| **the network's own depth** | perfect | 2.139 | 14.9 % |
| **the network's own depth** | optical flow | 2.233 | 23.1 % |

**With only a scale error the estimator is essentially exact: 0.9 %.** Add the shape error
the network actually has, and it fails its threshold *even with perfect correspondences*.
The real predictions land where that row predicts.

So the blocker is not the matching, and not the drift. **It is the 5.6 % shape error that
round 6 reported as good news.** A single number per view cannot absorb an error that
varies across the image, and the least squares pushes what it cannot fit into the scale.

## The plan, which is the only verdict that counts

Fused and planned against the flat's truth of 51.8 m² in three rooms, 48 views:

| | rooms | walls | area |
|---|---|---|---|
| exact depth | 2 | 5 | 41.2 m² (−21 %) |
| the network's depth, uncorrected | 2 | 7 | 13.6 m² (−74 %) |
| the network's depth, corrected | 1 | 6 | 1.6 m² (−97 %) |
| injected scale error, corrected with perfect matches | 2 | 6 | 27.8 m² (−46 %) |

**Correcting makes it worse.** And the last row is the ceiling: even a correction good to
0.9 % median, with a tail of 4.6 %, only reaches −46 % where exact depth reaches −21 %. A
few per cent of per-view scale error is worth twenty-five points of area, which says how
brittle the fusion is, and it is why the average improving is not enough.

## What the control had wrong, and what the card was spent on

The first control injected an *independent* scale per view. One 42-second GPU run, dumping
what the network predicts for the same 48 frames, showed that is not how the error behaves:

| the network's per-view scale | |
|---|---|
| autocorrelation of its log at one step | **+0.70** |
| at two steps / three steps | +0.36 / +0.08 |
| step-to-step change (median abs log) | **0.055** |
| the same if the views were independent | 0.238 |

**The scale drifts, it does not jump.** The first control was four times harsher per step
than reality, which is why it destroyed the plan (−94 %) where the network only reaches
−74 %. Re-run faithfully, with the drift and the shape error, it reproduces the real
result. A smoothness prior tying neighbouring scales together was added and helps a little
(21.6 % → 14.3 % median error) but comes nowhere near the threshold.

Two other things the round had to correct about itself:

- **Matching with the depth being estimated is circular**, and measurably so: it gave a
  spread of 3.4 against 1.1 for the same solver with honest matches. The first version of
  this experiment did exactly that.
- **A filter bug in the instrument** let masked predictions enter the system as zeros and
  drove an estimated scale to 0.18. It was caught because 0.18 is absurd, not because
  anything failed.

One measurement in this round produced nothing: dumping with `--overlap 4` gave a
byte-identical result, because `predict_views` does not apply the overlap; the chunking
layer above it does, and the dump bypasses that. So the difference between the 0.83 median
scale measured here and the 0.54 of round 6 is still unattributed: different frames (the
first 48 of the walk against 94 chosen for sharpness) and a different chunking path, and no
experiment yet separates them. That is the next thing to measure, and it matters, because
one of those two configurations is losing a third of the scale.
