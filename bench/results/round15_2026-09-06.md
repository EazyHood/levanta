# Round 15: the fit never claimed those cells, because the wall was only seen at its base

Round 14 established the mechanism of the leak: 61 % of the crossing front is a real wall
levanta did not draw, and 80 % of those cells have wall points within 0.25 m. The crude
remedy, cutting every ray at any wall point, was rejected by the bench. Before trying a
smarter blocker, one question decides whether it has anything to hold on to: **do those
cells sit on a fitted plane before any room-based trimming?**

## 1. No. Not on a face, not on a line, not on anything trimmed later

The same 116-view plan, the same 399 cells (real wall, not drawn), checked against every
stage that precedes the room-based cleanup:

| within 0.25 m of … | cells | share |
|---|---|---|
| a raw face, `t0..t1` (30 faces) | 4 | 1 % |
| a line interval before `tidy` (23 lines) | **0** | **0 %** |
| a line extended 1 m past its ends | 66 | 16.5 % |
| the fitted plane, unbounded | 231 | 58 % |
| **control:** the 61 cells with a *drawn* wall, against line intervals | | **97 %** |

The control says the instrument works. So `tidy` did not remove these walls: **they were
never fitted.** The 58 % on the unbounded plane means the right wall *direction and offset*
exist further along the same line; the extent simply stops before the leak. Extending 1 m
recovers only a sixth of it, so the un-fitted stretch is long.

## 2. Why: the evidence there is low, and the detector demands height

The wall-band points near those cells, followed through the height-coverage filter that
feeds face extraction (a 10 cm cell keeps its points only if they span enough height
bands: 3.5 of the 14.1 available here):

| of the 399 cells | cells | share |
|---|---|---|
| any wall-band point within 0.25 m | 333 | 83.5 % |
| a point that **survived** the filter within 0.25 m | 164 | 41 % |
| points present, **all of them filtered out** | 169 | 42 % |

And what the surviving evidence looks like there, against the rest of the flat:

| at the leak front | elsewhere |
|---|---|
| **6 height bands** seen per cell (median), p90 7 | **12** |
| points at **0.21–0.89 m** above the floor (p10–p90), median 0.51 m | |

**The camera saw the bottom half-metre of those walls and nothing above it.** Half the
points are discarded for not spanning enough height; the other half pass the filter but
are still too thin for a face. The tracing, meanwhile, asks for no height at all: a sight
line past that stretch marks it free. The detector demands height, the tracing demands
nothing, and the interior falls through the gap between the two rules.

This also fits the number the rounds keep circling: 24 m of walking left floor points on
46 % of the floor. A camera at eye height, tilted down to see the floor, catches the base
of the far wall and not its face.

## 3. What this does and does not license

- **Blocking rays on pre-trim planes**, the variant proposed after round 14, has nothing to
  grab: 0 of 399. Not implemented, on the strength of that number.
- Two levers remain, and both have to be measured before either is spent as the round's one
  planner change:
  1. **let low evidence block free space** without letting it draw a wall: a cell with
     wall-band points at several distinct stations, even in few height bands, is enough to
     stop a sight line, and not enough to become a wall. The doorway/furniture risk that
     sank round 14's version is smaller because furniture rarely forms a consistent line;
  2. **fit faces with a lower height requirement**, so a wall seen only at its base still
     becomes a wall. Riskier: it is exactly how a sofa becomes a wall.
- The capture guide's existing advice ("film the floor where it meets the wall") is right
  for the area and, on this evidence, wrong on its own for the walls: the same shot has to
  tilt up. That line goes into the guide in the next round together with whichever lever
  the bench accepts, so the advice and the detector agree on what a wall needs.

No planner change this round. Nothing implemented; one measurement, one localisation.
