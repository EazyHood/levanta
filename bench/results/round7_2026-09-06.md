# Round 7: the total was hiding two errors, and they are not the same error

## 1. Every room against its own truth

`bench/planner_bench.py` now prints, per scene, each true room against the levanta room
that covers most of it. The pairing is by area overlap on the true room's *territory*, not
by a centre falling inside a polygon, and the areas are levanta's own metres, never
rescaled by the alignment, so they still carry its metric error and still add up to the
total column.

| scene | room (truth) | levanta | error | of the room covered | off the floor |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 41069021 | 19.1 m² | 13.1 m² | −31 % | 66 % | 8 % |
| 42897526 | 5.6 m² | 4.7 m² | −17 % | 65 % | 29 % |
| 45260905 | 25.6 m² | 17.6 m² | −31 % | 87 % | 26 % |
| 47331964 | 17.9 m² | 13.0 m² | −28 % | 47 % | 12 % |
| 47331964 | 9.9 m² | not found | — | 15 % | — |
| 47430051 ⚠ | 5.4 m² | not found | — | 0 % | — |
| Replica apt_0 | 18.1 m² | 4.9 m² | −73 % | 27 % | 0 % |
| Replica apt_0 | 17.1 m² | 44.6 m² | **+161 %** | 44 % | **68 %** |
| Replica apt_0 | 16.6 m² | 12.0 m² | −28 % | 72 % | 1 % |

**The flat's +19 % was two errors that cancel, and the diagnosis was wrong twice over.**
It is not one room eating another: no detected room holds two true rooms. It is one room
of 4.9 m² where 18.1 m² should be, and one of 44.6 m² of which **68 % lies where the flat
has no floor at all**. Three rooms was the right count for the wrong reason.

The five one-room scenes are the other regime and they are consistent: −17 to −31 %, with
the room covered 65-87 %. There the outline is simply short.

## 2. Why a room goes missing: two scenes, two different answers

The bench now scores the real walls in two groups: the **partitions**, the walls with floor
from two different rooms within a metre, and the facade, everything else. Missing one is
not the same failure as missing the other.

| | partition recall | facade recall | rooms |
|---|---|---|---|
| 47331964 | **0 %** | 15 % | 1 of 2 |
| Replica apt_0 (perfect cloud) | **53 %** | 28 % | 3 of 3, geometry wrong |

- **47331964: the partition is never drawn.** Not one cell of it. There is no polygon to
  fail to close; the second room cannot exist. The fix is upstream of `build_rooms`.
- **The flat: the partitions are drawn, and better than the facade.** Here the failure is
  the closing, exactly as `debug.rooms` said in round 6: two pockets, none closed, two
  rooms from the seen-floor fallback.

So the question "not detected, or detected and not closed" has both answers, one per
regime, and a single fix cannot serve them.

## 3. The scene levanta refuses to stand behind, reported apart

47430051 reconstructs 3 % of its walls and levanta stamps it NOT RECONSTRUCTIBLE. Its
−100 % is the warning working, not the planner failing, so it no longer moves the averages.

    6 scenes levanta stands behind: area error 20 % on average, wall recall 34 %, rooms 7 of 8
    apart, flagged unreliable by levanta itself: 47430051, area −100 %, wall recall 3 %

Carrying that flag needed a fix: `FloorPlan.unreliable` reads `chunk_scales` and
`mask_fraction`, which the *reconstruction* wrote and which replanning a saved cloud does
not have. The bench was reading a flag that was always false. It now restores both numbers
from the run that produced each cloud, so the filter is levanta's own judgement and not
mine, and a change that silences the warning shows up here.

## 4. The audit that nearly did not happen

"68 % of a room off the floor" accuses the planner, so the first suspect had to be the
instrument. Four controls, all passed:

| control | result |
|---|---|
| does my truth lose floor? | 51.8 m² kept of 52.9 m² rasterised, one connected piece, no hole over 1 m² |
| is the alignment honest? | scale 1.0000, camera RMS 0.0000 m |
| do the cloud's own floor points land on the mesh? | 96 % of them |
| are levanta's rasters read the right way round? | they are `[row = y, col = x]`; my first pass had them transposed and I redid it |

The transposition is the same one that cost me a wrong reading in round 5. It was caught
here only because the control on the cloud failed loudly.

**And it turned up two things about the benchmark itself that round 6 stated too
confidently:**

- **`apartment_0` has a second storey**, 2.85 m above the one I call the truth: 45.5 m² of
  up-facing floor and 177 m² of wall above +1.5 m. The walk glimpses it (9 % of the cloud
  is above 2.5 m) and the plan has nowhere to put it. 10.1 m² of levanta's interior lands
  on that upper storey and nowhere on the lower one.
- **A perfect cloud is exact, not complete.** The camera path passes within 2 m of 80-94 %
  of every room, and floor points still reach only **36 %** of the flat's floor; everything
  below 2.5 m covers 53 %. Furniture and a camera at 1.5 m do that, and a phone would do
  the same. So "the planner's own ceiling" was the wrong phrase for this scene: it is the
  planner without network error, not the planner with full information. Both docstrings
  that said it have been corrected.

## 5. The rule that cost two rounds, written down

`CONTRIBUTING.md`, next to the other ground rules, and at the top of the bench itself:

> **One planner change per round.** Change one thing, run `bench/planner_bench.py`, keep it
> only if the table says so. Round 4 shipped two changes together; round 6 swept them apart
> and found one was worse or equal on all seven scenes and had been the thing that broke the
> published TUM example. A run over the sum of two changes cannot say which one earned the
> gain.

with its companion, which this round is the reason for: **judge a change room by room, not
on the total.**
