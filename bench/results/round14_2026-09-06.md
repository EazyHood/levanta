# Round 14: the tracing walks through walls it already has evidence for

The +162 % room leaves the building along a broad front. Round 13 killed two explanations
(no neck to cut, not rays to the storey above). This is the third, and it holds.

## 1. The crossing front, classified twice

Where the culprit room crosses out of the flat there are 654 grid cells, 32.7 m of
frontage. Each was checked against the mesh's real walls and against the walls levanta
drew:

| at the crossing front | cells | share |
|---|---|---|
| a real wall, and levanta drew it | 61 | 9 % |
| **a real wall, and levanta did not draw it** | **399** | **61 %** |
| no real wall and none drawn (a genuine opening) | 194 | 30 % |
| no real wall, but levanta drew one | 0 | 0 % |

**Six times in ten the interior leaves through a wall that is really there.** The tolerance
is if anything generous to the "drawn" side (half the wall thickness plus 0.15 m), so the
61 % is a floor, not a ceiling.

## 2. But not for the reason the hypothesis assumed

The hypothesis came from the TUM example, where a glass wall returned no depth. That cannot
be it here: this is a rendered walk and the depth is exact everywhere. So of the 399 cells
with an undrawn real wall:

| | cells | share |
|---|---|---|
| **wall points within 0.25 m — seen, and not drawn** | **318** | **80 %** |
| no wall point anywhere near — never observed | 81 | 20 % |

So it is neither glass nor a gap in the capture. **levanta already has the points.** A
sight line only knows where *it* ended, so a ray aimed past the edge of a wall walks
straight through that wall's cells and marks them free, while another ray's points sit in
exactly those cells.

## 3. The obvious remedy, implemented and rejected

`free_space_raster` now takes an `occupied` raster and cuts every ray at the first cell it
marks (`PlanOptions.free_blocked_by_walls`, default off). Feeding it the cells that hold
wall points:

| | mean area error | wall recall | rooms matched | TUM example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **today** | **17 %** | **31 %** | 6 of 8 | 24.5 m² (+0 %) |
| rays cut at wall points | 21 % | 25 % | 6 of 8 | **22.4 m² (−9 %)** |

It does what it was meant to do on the culprit room, which shrinks from +163 % to +132 %
with its off-floor share rising to 73 %. And it costs more elsewhere than it saves: one
scene drops from 4 walls to 1, wall recall falls six points, a room is lost on the flat,
and the published TUM example breaks by 9 %.

The reason is the same one that makes the leak possible: **the free-space raster is the
signal the rooms and doors are built from.** Blocking it at every cell that holds a wall
point starves the thing being fixed, because points sit in doorways and on furniture too.

So the default does not move. The mechanism is established and the crude version of the fix
is measured and rejected; the next attempt has to block on *strong* evidence only, not on
any point, and it has one bench row it must not break.

## 4. The disk, looked at once

Storage Sense is **on** (`01`=1), set to run automatically (`02`=1), to **delete temporary
files apps are not using** (`04`=1) and to empty the recycle bin of anything older than 30
days (`08`=1, `256`=30). Downloads cleanup is off.

That is a legitimate Windows cleanup and it fits the timeline: the disk fell to 24 GB, which
is the low-space condition that triggers it, and then rose to 51 and 74 GB with nobody
deleting anything. The recycle bin is empty and user temp is down to 0.56 GB.

The consequence worth passing on: **anything a session leaves in `%TEMP%` can be swept while
that session still needs it** — background task outputs, scratchpads, intermediate renders.
One task output file in this session vanished mid-run today. Work in progress does not
belong in temp on this machine.
